WORLD NEWS · WHY IT IS HERE

We do not dump the whole newspaper onto this page. A story makes the list only if it could change fuel prices, borrowing costs, or construction work.

1. Talks about the Iran war and oil shipments. Iran said it could reopen the main oil shipping lane in about a week if the U.S. eases pressure. Saudi Arabia is also testing a pipeline that can move oil without that lane. Oil fell on those headlines.

Why it matters here: cheaper oil usually means cheaper diesel and asphalt. That helps a job. The war is not over. One headline can reverse this.

2. The Fed already raised rates last week, and more hikes are on the table. Officials keep saying inflation is not finished. The 10-year borrowing rate is still near 5%.

Why it matters here: when the government rate stays high, mortgages and project loans stay expensive. That leans against new houses and some equipment orders.

3. World leaders are in New York this week. President Trump spoke at the U.N. and is meeting other leaders. China’s president is due in the U.S. later this week.

Why it matters here: we do not trade speeches. We watch whether oil or the 10-year rate actually move after the meetings.

WHAT THE MARKET DID

Monday: stocks jumped. Oil and borrowing costs eased.

Tuesday: stocks held near their highs. Oil eased again. The 10-year rate sat around 4.96%.

Plain English: money is still expensive, fuel is less scary than last week, and nobody needed to spend the play money.

WHAT WE DO NEXT Wednesday morning we check oil and the 10-year first. If both are quiet, cash stays cash. A speech is not a reason to buy Caterpillar.
